\subsection{Effect of temperature}
\label{subsec:temperature}

Figure~\ref{fig:temperature} compares the tests of the temperature series on the mixture with $w = 0.33$: F212 at $-10\,^\circ$C, F213 and F214 at $-20\,^\circ$C and F218 at $-30\,^\circ$C. In F213 and F214 the cooling unit lost control of the temperature during the test (Section~\ref{sec:temperature_events}); only the parts before these events are used. F218 ran for 42.5\,h without any temperature event, with an LVDT scale of 0.98 against the caliper.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{Images/fig_temperature_series.pdf}
    \caption{Void ratio against time under 2\,MPa for the mixture with $w = 0.33$ at different temperatures. Time measured from the moment the oil pressure reached 1.9\,MPa. Solid and dashed: valid part of each test; dotted: after the loss of temperature control.}
    \label{fig:temperature}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The two tests at $-20\,^\circ$C agree.} F213 and F214 differ by about 0.01 in void ratio over the valid part of both tests (Table~\ref{tab:temperature_e}), which gives the repeatability of the procedure. An error of 0.5\,mm in the sample height corresponds to about 0.02 in void ratio.
    \item \textbf{From $-20$ to $-10\,^\circ$C the creep is much faster.} The time needed to accumulate a given decrease in void ratio after the first minute is 3.5--6 times shorter at $-10\,^\circ$C (Table~\ref{tab:temperature_times}), and the curve at $-10\,^\circ$C bends downwards in $\log t$, with $C_\alpha$ increasing from 0.05 to 0.12, while at $-20\,^\circ$C it stays linear with $C_\alpha\approx0.04$. The curves at $-10$ and $-20\,^\circ$C overlap for the first 10\,min, as expected if this part of the compaction is due to rearrangement of the regolith skeleton (Section~\ref{subsec:mechanisms}).
    \item \textbf{From $-20$ to $-30\,^\circ$C the creep is slower, with the same $C_\alpha$.} F218 is linear in $\log t$ with $C_\alpha = 0.042$, the same slope as at $-20\,^\circ$C, and lies 0.02--0.035 above the two tests at $-20\,^\circ$C (Table~\ref{tab:temperature_e}). With the same slope, this offset is a shift of the curve along the time axis: the same void ratio is reached about 3--5 times later at $-30\,^\circ$C.
\end{itemize}

The ratio between $-10$ and $-20\,^\circ$C corresponds to an apparent activation energy of roughly 60--130\,kJ/mol, depending on the scale adopted for F213 and on the stage of the test. Values in the upper part of this range are expected above about $-15\,^\circ$C, where the activation energy of ice increases (Section~\ref{sec:ice_rheology}). The factor of 3--5 between $-20$ and $-30\,^\circ$C corresponds to about 55--85\,kJ/mol, in the range of the creep mechanisms of ice below $-15\,^\circ$C. This value rests on a single test at $-30\,^\circ$C, and the offset is already present at 10\,min, so part of it could come from a difference in the initial state of the samples. The temperature-step test F219, in which the same sample is held at $-30$, $-20$ and $-10\,^\circ$C in sequence under 2\,MPa, removes the sample-to-sample scatter from this comparison.

\paragraph{Test at $-40\,^\circ$C.} Test F215 is not used. The LVDT stopped following the piston, the cooling unit did not hold the set temperature, and the final piston position could not be measured with confidence. With the best estimate of the final position the sample would have reached $e\approx0.20$, far below the value of 0.39 reached at $-20\,^\circ$C by the same mixture (F213) and close to that of the sample with three times as much ice (F209). A sample that compacts more at $-40$ than at $-20\,^\circ$C is not credible, so either the final height or the temperature is wrong. The lowest temperature of the present programme is therefore $-30\,^\circ$C.

\subsection{Effect of ice content}
\label{subsec:ice_content}

Figure~\ref{fig:ice_content} shows the void ratio during the hold at 2\,MPa for the tests of the ice-content series. The ice content decides whether, and how fast, the sample keeps compacting after loading.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{Images/fig_ice_content_series.pdf}
    \caption{Void ratio against time under 2\,MPa at $-20\,^\circ$C for LHS-1 with different ice contents. Curves anchored to the measured initial and final heights; F209 and F211 from the oil volume; small noise reversals removed. For $w = 0.33$ the reference test F213 is shown up to the loss of temperature control, and the first test F207 dashed.}
    \label{fig:ice_content}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The threshold is low.} Without ice $C_\alpha\approx0.0016$; with $w = 0.10$ it is already 0.04--0.05, with $w = 0.20$ 0.044--0.059, with $w = 0.33$ about 0.04 (F213) and with $w = 1.0$ about 0.10.
    \item \textbf{The shape of the curve changes.} F210, F211 and F213 are almost linear in $\log t$, like the secondary compression of a soil. F209 bends downwards, like densifying firn, and ends at $e = 0.17$ (porosity about 15\%), close to pore close-off. The first test at $w = 0.33$ (F207) also bent downwards, while its repeat F213 did not; the difference is not yet explained and is one reason to treat F207 as less reliable.
    \item \textbf{The rates are those of ice.} The axial strain rates at 100\,min are between 1 and $3\times10^{-6}$\,s$^{-1}$, close to the rate of about $3\times10^{-6}$\,s$^{-1}$ predicted by Equation~\eqref{eq:gk} for dense ice at 2\,MPa and $-20\,^\circ$C.
\end{itemize}
Since $C_\alpha$ alone hides the effect of the different initial void ratios, the rates are also compared at equal void ratio (Table~\ref{tab:equal_e}). At $e = 0.45$ the rate of compaction increases by more than two orders of magnitude between $\phi_i = 0.38$ and $\phi_i = 0.75$. The tests reach this void ratio at very different times, F209 only two minutes after loading, so the comparison mixes the effect of ice content with that of the stage of the test; the trend is nevertheless clear.

\subsection{Effect of axial stress}
\label{subsec:stress}

In test F216 the mixture with $w = 0.33$ was loaded at $-20\,^\circ$C in four steps of oil pressure, 0.5, 1, 2 and 3.4\,MPa, each held for about 10.8\,h (Figure~\ref{fig:stress_steps}). The LVDT followed the piston during the whole test, with a scale of 0.97 against the caliper, and no temperature event was detected. During the free run of the piston the oil pressure was 150--185\,kPa, which gives a seal friction of about 170\,kPa in this test.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$C_\alpha$ does not depend on stress.} After the first half hour every step is linear in $\log t$ with $C_\alpha\approx0.045$, the value found in the tests loaded directly to 2\,MPa (F211, F213).
    \item \textbf{At equal time the compression is linear in $\log\sigma$.} The void ratios at the end of the four steps lie on a line with $C_c = 0.31$, close to the compression index of the dry regolith (0.27--0.30, Section~\ref{sec:results_dry}).
    \item \textbf{The rate at equal void ratio depends strongly on stress.} Extrapolating the relation between rate and void ratio of each step to the void ratio of the next, doubling the oil pressure multiplies the rate by about 200--500 from 0.5 to 1\,MPa and by about 85--115 from 1 to 2\,MPa; the last step (a factor 1.7 in pressure) multiplies it by about 18--27. The apparent stress exponent is between about 5.5 and 9 in terms of oil pressure, and between about 5 and 7 after subtracting the seal friction. The first step, where the friction is a third of the oil pressure, is the least reliable.
    \item \textbf{The loading path has little effect.} After 1900\,min from the start, of which the last 650 at 2\,MPa, the void ratio in F216 was 0.407; F213, loaded directly to 2\,MPa, was at 0.409 at the same time.
\end{itemize}
The initial height of the sample is less certain than in the other tests: the oil pressure started to rise after about half of the gap expected from a height of 45\,mm, so that $e_0$ lies between 0.80 and 0.90. This does not affect the results above, which are anchored to the final height.

\section{Dry regolith}
\label{sec:results_dry}
% Reference test for the skeleton-only end-member; comparison with literature oedometric data.

Test F208 was run on 32\,g of dry LHS-1 poured loose to a height of 47.55\,mm ($e_0 = 1.07$, dry density 1.33\,g/cm$^3$). Almost all the compaction took place during loading: the void ratio fell to 0.44 within about a minute, and more than half of this change occurred below 0.4\,MPa of oil pressure, where the response is dominated by seal friction and collapse of the loose packing. Between 0.5 and 2\,MPa the compression index is $C_c\approx0.27$--0.30. During the following 41\,h at 2\,MPa the void ratio decreased by only 0.005 ($C_\alpha\approx0.0016$), and the oil volume increased only by the amount of the leak. On unloading the piston did not move back: seal friction prevents any measurable rebound. The final height of 32.88\,mm gives $e = 0.43$ and a dry density of 1.92\,g/cm$^3$.

\section{Post-test observations}
\label{sec:results_posttest}
% Final height and mass, mass gained by the frits, visual appearance, microstructure if available.

The clean, dry frits weigh about 3.75\,g each. After F209 each frit had gained about 0.25\,g, and after F210 only 0.025\,g. The final mass of F210 (28.6\,g) matched the mass placed in the vessel. Masses are now weighed to 0.01\,g immediately after extraction, to limit frost deposition on the cold sample and frits. Even so, the samples of F211--F213 weighed 0.7--1.3\,g more than the material placed in the vessel, which is attributed to frost; the initial masses are therefore used to compute $H_s$. The frits gained at most 0.13\,g in these tests.

\section{Summary of experimental findings}
\label{sec:results_summary}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Dry LHS-1 compacts during loading and then stops: the time-dependent compaction of the mixtures is due to the ice.
    \item A small amount of ice ($w = 0.10$) is enough to produce creep with $C_\alpha\approx0.04$; at equal void ratio the rate increases strongly with the ice fraction.
    \item The creep is thermally activated, with the same $C_\alpha\approx0.04$ at $-30$ and $-20\,^\circ$C. Between $-30$ and $-20\,^\circ$C it becomes about 3--5 times faster (apparent activation energy 55--85\,kJ/mol, one test at $-30\,^\circ$C); between $-20$ and $-10\,^\circ$C it becomes 3.5--6 times faster and starts to accelerate in $\log t$.
    \item Two tests under the same conditions (F213 and F214, $-20\,^\circ$C) agree to about 0.01 in void ratio.
    \item The measured strain rates are of the same order as those of dense ice from the law of Goldsby and Kohlstedt.
    \item In the stress-step test $C_\alpha\approx0.045$ at all stresses and the void ratio at equal time is linear in $\log\sigma$ with $C_c\approx0.31$, close to the dry regolith. At equal void ratio the apparent stress exponent is about 5--7, higher than that of any creep mechanism of ice.
    \item The test at $-40\,^\circ$C failed and is excluded; the temperature series rests on one test per temperature below and above $-20\,^\circ$C and is completed by the temperature-step test F219.
\end{itemize}
